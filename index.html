The name

Nightshade, like the family tree.

Peppers, tomatillos, tomatoes, potatoes — the nightshade family is where nearly every good hot sauce starts.

So we took the family name and grew it into a label. Since 2024, every batch has been a small, numbered experiment: ferment the fruit and chiles, taste it with the market crowd, bottle what works — and when the batch is gone, it's gone.

The blossom on the label is the nightshade flower itself: quiet, hardy, a lot more interesting than it looks. That's the idea.

The method

Slow food, fast shelf.

01

Ferment

Every bottle starts as a ferment — the label says so, and it's the whole point. Time, salt patience, and chiles doing what chiles do.

02

Keep it small

Batches stay deliberately small so each one can be tuned, tasted, and adjusted. No batch is too precious to rework if it isn't right.

03

Number & pour

Finished batches get their experiment number and a spot on the market table. Tasters decide; sell-outs decide faster.
